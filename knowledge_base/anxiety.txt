ANXIETY AND MENTAL HEALTH DISORDERS
=====================================

Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD)
------------------------------------
Definition:
GAD is characterised by persistent, excessive, and difficult-to-control worry about multiple
everyday concerns, lasting ≥6 months, causing significant impairment in daily functioning.

Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5):
- Excessive anxiety and worry (apprehensive expectation) occurring more days than not for ≥6 months
- Difficulty controlling worry
- Associated with ≥3 of 6 symptoms:
  1. Restlessness or feeling keyed-up or on edge
  2. Being easily fatigued
  3. Difficulty concentrating or mind going blank
  4. Irritability
  5. Muscle tension
  6. Sleep disturbance
- Significant distress or functional impairment
- Not attributable to substance/medication or another medical condition

Assessment Tools:
- GAD-7 (Generalised Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale):
  Score 0–4: minimal anxiety
  Score 5–9: mild anxiety
  Score 10–14: moderate anxiety
  Score ≥15: severe anxiety
- PHQ-9: assess co-morbid depression

Screening for organic causes:
- Thyroid function tests (hyperthyroidism mimics anxiety)
- Caffeine intake, substance use (cannabis, stimulants)
- Medication review (corticosteroids, beta-agonists, decongestants)
- FBC, glucose (anaemia, hypoglycaemia)

Panic Disorder
--------------
- Recurrent, unexpected panic attacks (sudden surge of intense fear peaking within minutes)
- Physical symptoms: palpitations, chest tightness, dyspnoea, dizziness, paresthesia, sweating, tremor
- Agoraphobia: fear/avoidance of situations where escape may be difficult
- Persistent worry about future attacks ≥1 month

Treatment Approach (Stepped Care Model)
-----------------------------------------
Step 1: Watchful waiting, self-help resources, psychoeducation
- Advise: lifestyle changes, sleep hygiene, reduction of caffeine and alcohol
- Provide written information

Step 2: Low-intensity psychological interventions
- Guided self-help based on CBT principles
- Computerised CBT (cCBT) programmes

Step 3: High-intensity psychological treatment
- Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT): Gold standard — 12–15 sessions
  Techniques: cognitive restructuring, exposure, relaxation training, worry time
- Applied relaxation (for GAD)

Step 4: Pharmacological Treatment

First-line Pharmacotherapy:
1. SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors):
   - Sertraline 50mg OD (up to 200mg); escitalopram 10mg OD (up to 20mg)
   - Paroxetine: effective but more discontinuation effects
   - Treatment response: 4–6 weeks; full benefit 8–12 weeks
   - Continue for 12 months after remission to prevent relapse

2. SNRIs (Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors):
   - Venlafaxine 75mg OD (up to 225mg); duloxetine 30mg OD (up to 120mg)
   - Effective for both anxiety and comorbid depression

Second-line:
- Pregabalin 150–600mg/day (in 2–3 divided doses): approved for GAD; rapid onset
  Caution: dependence potential; withdrawal; cognitive impairment
- Buspirone: non-addictive; slow onset (2–4 weeks); suitable for chronic GAD
- TCAs (imipramine, clomipramine): effective but poorly tolerated

Benzodiazepines:
- Only for short-term use (≤2–4 weeks) during acute crises
- NOT recommended for long-term treatment due to tolerance, dependence, and cognitive impairment
- Diazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam

Lifestyle Interventions (Evidence-based):
- Regular aerobic exercise (150 min/week): as effective as medication for mild-moderate anxiety
- Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR): reduces rumination and anxiety
- Adequate sleep hygiene: consistent bedtime, no screens before sleep, dark/cool room
- Dietary: reduce caffeine; maintain regular meal times; limit alcohol
- Social support networks

Anxiety with Comorbidities:
- Depression (most common comorbidity): 50% of GAD patients — combined treatment
- Somatic symptoms: pain, fatigue, GI symptoms — need integrated approach
- Substance use disorders: treat concurrently
- Cardiac disease: rule out arrhythmias before diagnosing anxiety

Depression (Major Depressive Disorder)
---------------------------------------
Core features (PHQ-9):
- Depressed mood most of the day
- Anhedonia (loss of interest or pleasure)
- ≥5 symptoms ≥2 weeks

Additional symptoms:
- Weight change/appetite disturbance
- Insomnia or hypersomnia
- Psychomotor agitation or retardation
- Fatigue, loss of energy
- Feelings of worthlessness/guilt
- Difficulty concentrating
- Recurrent thoughts of death/suicidal ideation

Risk Assessment — ALWAYS assess suicide risk:
- Active suicidal ideation: intent, plan, means
- Past attempts, family history
- Protective factors: social support, religious beliefs, children
- Immediate safety planning if at risk

Treatment of Depression:
- Mild: psychosocial interventions, CBT, behavioural activation
- Moderate-severe: antidepressant + CBT
- SSRIs are first-line: fluoxetine, sertraline, citalopram, escitalopram
- Continue antidepressant ≥6 months after remission; 2 years if recurrent
- ECT (electroconvulsive therapy): severe/refractory depression or if life-threatening

Red Flags for Urgent Psychiatric Referral:
- Active suicidal ideation with intent/plan
- Psychotic features (hallucinations, delusions)
- Severe self-neglect
- Catatonia
- First episode of mania or hypomania
