HEADACHE AND MIGRAINE
======================

Classification of Headache Disorders (ICHD-3)
----------------------------------------------
Primary headaches (no underlying structural disease):
1. Migraine (with and without aura)
2. Tension-type headache (TTH) — most common
3. Cluster headache and other trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias (TACs)

Secondary headaches (symptom of underlying disease):
- SAH, meningitis, raised ICP, giant cell arteritis, hypertensive emergency

Approach to Headache — Red Flags (SNOOP4 Mnemonic):
S — Systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss, cancer/HIV history)
N — Neurological signs or symptoms
O — Onset sudden (thunderclap — SAH until proven otherwise)
O — Onset after 50 years (first headache in elderly — GCA, malignancy)
P — Pattern change (new headache or change in existing pattern)
P — Precipitated by Valsalva (coughing, straining — raised ICP)
P — Postural component (worse lying flat — raised ICP; better lying flat — low CSF pressure)
P — Pregnancy/puerperium (pre-eclampsia, cerebral venous thrombosis)

MIGRAINE
=========

Epidemiology:
- Prevalence: 12% population; female:male ratio 3:1
- Peak onset: 25–55 years
- Leading cause of disability in working-age adults

Migraine Without Aura (Common Migraine):
Diagnostic criteria (at least 5 attacks):
- Duration 4–72 hours (untreated or unsuccessfully treated)
- ≥2 of: unilateral, pulsating, moderate/severe intensity, aggravated by physical activity
- ≥1 of: nausea/vomiting, photophobia AND phonophobia

Migraine With Aura (Classic Migraine):
- Visual aura most common: positive (scintillating scotoma, fortification spectra) or
  negative (scotoma) visual phenomena developing gradually over 5–20 minutes, lasting <60 minutes
- Sensory aura: unilateral paresthesia spreading (cheiro-oral)
- Dysphasia (language disturbance)
- Motor aura (hemiplegic migraine — rare)

Phases of Migraine:
1. Prodrome (hours-days before): yawning, food cravings, mood changes, fatigue, photosensitivity
2. Aura (30–60 minutes)
3. Headache phase (4–72 hours)
4. Postdrome: "migraine hangover" — cognitive blunting, fatigue, neck stiffness

Migraine Triggers (common, patient-specific):
- Menstruation (most reliable trigger)
- Sleep deprivation or oversleeping
- Stress and stress relief (let-down migraine)
- Dietary: alcohol (red wine, beer), caffeine withdrawal, skipping meals
- Environmental: bright lights, strong smells, weather changes, altitude
- Hormonal: OCP, perimenstrual

Investigations:
- Clinical diagnosis; no routine investigations required for typical migraine
- Brain MRI: if first/worst headache, progressive headache, atypical aura, focal neurological signs,
  age >50 at onset, headache with exertion/Valsalva
- LP: if SAH suspected and CT head normal

Acute (Abortive) Treatment:
Step 1: Analgesics (mild-moderate attacks)
- Aspirin 900mg + metoclopramide 10mg (superior to paracetamol for migraine)
- Ibuprofen 400–600mg + antiemetic
- Paracetamol 1g + codeine (limited evidence)

Step 2: Triptans (moderate-severe attacks, or analgesic failure)
- Sumatriptan 50–100mg oral; 6mg SC; 10mg intranasal
- Zolmitriptan, rizatriptan, eletriptan, naratriptan
- MOA: 5HT1B/1D agonists — vasoconstriction, inhibit CGRP release
- Contraindications: established coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension, hemiplegic migraine
- Take early in headache phase (NOT in aura)
- Avoid overuse: medication overuse headache if used >10 days/month

Step 3: Antiemetics
- Metoclopramide 10mg or prochlorperazine 10mg (buccal/oral) at headache onset
- Also promotes gastric emptying (enhances analgesic absorption)
- Domperidone 10mg less extrapyramidal effects

Gepants (newer agents):
- Ubrogepant, rimegepant: CGRP receptor antagonists — safe in cardiovascular disease
- Lasmiditan: 5HT1F agonist — no vasoconstrictive effects

Preventive (Prophylactic) Treatment
-------------------------------------
Indications:
- ≥4 migraine attacks/month causing significant disability
- Attacks lasting >48 hours
- Triptan/analgesic overuse
- Hemiplegic, basilar, or frequent aura migraine

First-line options:
1. Propranolol 40–120mg BD (beta-blocker): evidence-based; avoid in asthma, depression
2. Topiramate 25–100mg OD: weight neutral/loss benefit; side effects: cognitive slowing, paraesthesia, teratogenic
3. Amitriptyline 10–75mg nocte: especially if comorbid insomnia or tension-type
4. Sodium valproate 400–600mg BD: effective but teratogenic (avoid women of childbearing potential)

CGRP Monoclonal Antibodies (newer, injectable):
- Erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab (monthly or quarterly SC injections)
- Highly effective, excellent tolerability; use if 2 oral preventives failed

Botulinum Toxin A (Botox):
- Licensed for chronic migraine (≥15 headache days/month, ≥8 of which are migraine)
- Administered every 12 weeks by specialist

Non-pharmacological Prevention:
- Regular sleep schedule, consistent mealtimes
- Maintain hydration
- Regular moderate exercise
- Trigger identification and avoidance (headache diary)
- Stress management: CBT, biofeedback, relaxation therapy
- Limit caffeine and alcohol

TENSION-TYPE HEADACHE (TTH)
============================
- Most prevalent headache (40–80% population)
- Character: pressing/tightening (band-like), bilateral, mild-moderate severity
- Duration: 30 minutes to 7 days
- NOT aggravated by routine physical activity
- Absent: nausea/vomiting, photophobia AND phonophobia (not both)

Management:
- Acute: aspirin 900mg or ibuprofen 400–600mg
- Paracetamol less effective than NSAIDs for TTH
- Avoid opioids and caffeine-containing products (medication overuse risk)
- Prevention: amitriptyline 10–75mg; manage stress, posture, sleep
- Physiotherapy: cervical manual therapy, muscle relaxation

CLUSTER HEADACHE
=================
- Male predominance (5:1); episodic or chronic
- Excruciating unilateral orbital/supraorbital/temporal pain (10/10)
- Duration: 15–180 minutes; 1–8 attacks/day; nocturnal
- Ipsilateral: lacrimation, nasal congestion/rhinorrhoea, ptosis, miosis, facial flushing

Acute: 100% oxygen (12–15 L/min for 15 minutes) — first-line; sumatriptan 6mg SC
Prevention: verapamil 240–480mg/day; lithium for chronic cluster

MEDICATION OVERUSE HEADACHE (MOH)
===================================
- Occurs with: triptans >10 days/month; analgesics/opioids >15 days/month
- Presents as: daily or near-daily headache, worse in morning
- Treatment: withdraw offending medication (supervised withdrawal); preventive therapy
