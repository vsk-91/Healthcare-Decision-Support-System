RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS
======================

Overview
--------
Respiratory tract infections (RTIs) are among the most common reasons for primary care consultations.
They are divided into upper RTI (URTI) and lower RTI (LRTI) and are predominantly viral in aetiology.

Upper Respiratory Tract Infections (URTI)
------------------------------------------
Includes: common cold, pharyngitis, tonsillitis, sinusitis, otitis media, laryngitis

Causative agents:
- Viral (>80%): rhinovirus (most common), influenza A/B, RSV, coronavirus, adenovirus, parainfluenza
- Bacterial: Group A Streptococcus (pharyngitis), Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae

Clinical features:
- Rhinorrhoea (nasal discharge), nasal congestion
- Sore throat, odynophagia
- Cough (initially dry, may become productive)
- Low-grade fever (<38.5°C typically in viral)
- Myalgia, malaise, headache
- Hoarseness (laryngitis)

CENTOR CRITERIA (pharyngitis — likelihood of Group A Strep):
Score 1 point each: tonsillar exudate, tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy, fever >38°C, absence of cough
- Score 0–1: antibiotics NOT indicated (low bacterial probability)
- Score 2–3: rapid antigen test recommended
- Score ≥4: consider empirical antibiotics

Lower Respiratory Tract Infections (LRTI)
------------------------------------------
Includes: acute bronchitis, pneumonia, exacerbations of COPD/asthma

Acute Bronchitis:
- Predominantly viral (influenza, RSV, rhinovirus)
- Self-limiting (7–10 days)
- Antibiotics NOT routinely indicated
- Symptomatic: antipyretics, adequate hydration, cough suppressants

Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)
------------------------------------
Common pathogens:
- Streptococcus pneumoniae (most common typical)
- Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Chlamydophila pneumoniae (atypical)
- Haemophilus influenzae (COPD patients)
- Staphylococcus aureus (post-influenza)
- Legionella pneumophila (water systems, travel)
- Viruses: influenza, SARS-CoV-2

Clinical features:
- Productive cough (purulent sputum, haemoptysis)
- Fever >38.5°C, rigors
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Dyspnoea, tachypnoea
- Dullness to percussion, reduced breath sounds, bronchial breathing, crepitations on auscultation

Investigations for Pneumonia:
- CXR: consolidation, infiltrates
- Sputum culture and sensitivity
- Blood cultures (if hospitalised)
- FBC: elevated WBC with neutrophilia (bacterial); lymphocytosis (viral)
- CRP, procalcitonin
- Urinary antigen: Streptococcus pneumoniae, Legionella
- Pulse oximetry, ABG if O2 sats <94%

Severity Assessment — CURB-65 Score:
Score 1 point each:
- Confusion (new)
- Urea >7 mmol/L
- Respiratory rate ≥30/min
- Blood pressure systolic <90 or diastolic ≤60 mmHg
- Age ≥65 years

CURB-65 0–1: treat at home
CURB-65 2: consider hospital admission
CURB-65 ≥3: hospital admission required; consider ICU if ≥4–5

Antibiotic Treatment for CAP:
Low severity (community):
- Amoxicillin 500mg TDS for 5 days (typical)
- Doxycycline 200mg loading then 100mg OD for 5 days (atypical/penicillin allergy)
- Clarithromycin 500mg BD for 5 days

Moderate severity (hospital):
- Amoxicillin + clarithromycin (to cover atypical)
- Co-amoxiclav 1.2g IV TDS + clarithromycin if aspiration suspected

Severe (ICU):
- Co-amoxiclav/piperacillin-tazobactam IV + clarithromycin or levofloxacin IV

Red Flags Requiring Urgent Evaluation:
----------------------------------------
- SpO2 <94% on room air
- Respiratory rate >30/min
- Systolic BP <90 mmHg
- Altered mental status
- Inability to swallow oral medications
- Haemoptysis
- Pleuritic chest pain suggesting empyema
- Stridor (upper airway obstruction)
- Age extremes (very young, very elderly)
- Immunocompromised state

Influenza
----------
- Sudden onset, high fever >38.5°C, myalgia, headache, dry cough
- Oseltamivir (Tamiflu) if within 48h of symptom onset; indicated in high-risk groups
- Annual influenza vaccination recommended for all ≥65 years, healthcare workers, high-risk groups

COVID-19 Considerations:
- Anosmia/ageusia (loss of smell/taste)
- Varied presentation from asymptomatic to severe ARDS
- Antivirals: nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid) in high-risk within 5 days of symptoms

Symptomatic Management (Viral URTI):
--------------------------------------
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen for fever/pain
- Adequate fluid intake (2–3 L/day)
- Saline nasal irrigation for congestion
- Honey for cough in adults (1–2 teaspoons)
- Decongestants (pseudoephedrine) short-term only; avoid in hypertension

Antibiotic Stewardship:
------------------------
- Antibiotics are NOT appropriate for viral URTI (common cold, influenza)
- Overuse of antibiotics leads to antibiotic resistance
- Use delayed prescribing strategy: provide prescription but advise to wait 48–72h
