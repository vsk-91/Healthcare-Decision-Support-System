PREVENTIVE MEDICINE AND HEALTH SCREENING
=========================================

Overview
--------
Preventive medicine aims to reduce the incidence, prevalence, and consequences of disease through
early detection (screening), immunisation, lifestyle modification, and risk factor management.
It encompasses primary prevention (preventing disease onset), secondary prevention (early detection),
and tertiary prevention (reducing disability from established disease).

KEY LIFESTYLE INTERVENTIONS
============================

Physical Activity
-----------------
WHO recommendations (adults 18–64 years):
- ≥150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week
  OR 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity
- Muscle-strengthening activities (resistance exercise): ≥2 days/week
- Reduce prolonged sedentary behaviour

Benefits of regular exercise:
- Reduces risk of: cardiovascular disease (35%), T2DM (50%), colon cancer (30%), breast cancer (20%)
- Reduces all-cause mortality by 30–35%
- Mental health: reduces depression and anxiety
- Bone health: osteoporosis prevention
- Weight management: each 1kg weight loss by exercise reduces CVD risk

Types of exercise:
- Aerobic (cardiovascular): walking, running, swimming, cycling — improves VO2max, reduces BP and lipids
- Resistance/strength training: reduces insulin resistance, improves muscle mass
- Flexibility: yoga, stretching — reduces injury risk
- Balance exercises: falls prevention in elderly

Nutrition and Diet
------------------
Mediterranean Diet (highest evidence for CVD prevention):
- High: olive oil, vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, fish
- Moderate: poultry, dairy
- Low: red meat, processed food, refined sugars
- Reduces CVD risk by 30%, T2DM by 20%, all-cause mortality

DASH Diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension):
- High in: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy
- Limits: saturated fat, sodium (<2300mg/day), added sugars
- Reduces systolic BP by 8–14 mmHg

General dietary principles:
- 5 portions fruit and vegetables daily
- Oily fish ≥2x/week (omega-3 fatty acids: sardines, mackerel, salmon)
- Reduce ultra-processed foods, ready meals, fast food
- Limit red meat (<70g/day); avoid processed meat (carcinogenic)
- Whole grains (bread, rice, pasta) over refined carbohydrates
- Adequate water intake: 6–8 glasses/day

Smoking Cessation
------------------
Smoking causes 50% of all avoidable deaths; increases risk of:
- Lung cancer: 15–25x increased risk
- CVD: 2–4x increased risk
- COPD: major cause
- 17 other cancer types

Benefits of quitting:
- After 48 hours: nicotine cleared from body
- After 1 year: CVD risk halved
- After 10 years: lung cancer risk halved
- After 15 years: CVD risk equivalent to non-smoker

Cessation strategies (highest success = combination):
1. Behavioural support (individual/group counselling)
2. Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT): patch, gum, inhaler, nasal spray — doubles quit rates
3. Varenicline (Champix): most effective pharmacotherapy (3x success rate); partial nicotine agonist
4. Bupropion: alternative to NRT; contraindicated in epilepsy, eating disorders
5. E-cigarettes: NICE 2021 endorses as cessation aid; evidence growing

Alcohol
-------
UK low-risk guidelines: ≤14 units/week; spread over ≥3 days; avoid binge drinking
1 unit = 10mL pure alcohol = half pint (3.5% beer), small glass wine (125mL, 9%), 25mL spirit

Health risks of excess alcohol:
- Liver disease (fatty liver, hepatitis, cirrhosis)
- 7 cancer types (mouth, throat, oesophagus, breast, bowel)
- Hypertension, cardiomyopathy
- Mental health: depression, anxiety, psychosis
- AUDIT-C screening questionnaire (score ≥5 men, ≥4 women suggests harmful use)

CANCER SCREENING
=================

Cervical Cancer Screening (Smear Test):
- UK: every 3 years (25–49 years); every 5 years (50–64 years)
- HPV primary testing + cytology if HPV positive
- HPV vaccination: Gardasil-9 (types 6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58) — school age girls and boys

Breast Cancer Screening:
- UK NHS mammography: every 3 years (50–70 years)
- Self-examination: not formally recommended but awareness encouraged
- High-risk: BRCA1/2 carriers — annual MRI from age 30

Colorectal Cancer Screening:
- UK: Faecal Immunochemical Test (FIT) kit, home-based, every 2 years (50–74 years)
- Positive FIT → colonoscopy
- Lynch syndrome/family history → earlier/more frequent colonoscopy

Prostate Cancer:
- PSA test available on request (NHS); no formal UK screening programme
- Controversially high false positive rate leading to overdiagnosis and overtreatment

Lung Cancer Screening (UK NLST equivalent):
- Low-dose CT: being rolled out for high-risk smokers (55–74 years, 30+ pack-year history)

CARDIOVASCULAR SCREENING
==========================
NHS Health Check (England):
- Age 40–74 years, no known cardiovascular disease
- Every 5 years
- Assesses: CVD risk (QRISK3), diabetes risk, BMI, BP, cholesterol, smoking, alcohol

Blood Pressure Monitoring:
- All adults >40 years: checked at least every 5 years
- If elevated: every year
- Home BP monitoring: more accurate than clinic measurement

Cholesterol Screening:
- Fasting lipid profile: total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides
- All adults with CVD risk factors; routine screening from age 40

Diabetes Screening:
- FPG or HbA1c: adults with risk factors (obesity, family history, hypertension, previous GDM)
- NHS Diabetes Prevention Programme: for pre-diabetes (impaired glucose)

IMMUNISATION (UK Routine Schedule Adults)
==========================================
- Influenza vaccine: annual — all ≥65 years, high-risk groups, healthcare workers
- Pneumococcal vaccine (PPV23): once at 65; once for clinical risk groups
- Shingles (herpes zoster): Shingrix 2-dose — age 70 (UK); 50+ (US)
- COVID-19: updated boosters for vulnerable groups (JCVI guidance)
- Td/IPV: tetanus booster if travelling to endemic areas or wound risk

OBESITY AND WEIGHT MANAGEMENT
================================
Definitions (BMI):
- Underweight: <18.5
- Healthy: 18.5–24.9
- Overweight: 25.0–29.9
- Obesity Class I: 30.0–34.9
- Obesity Class II: 35.0–39.9
- Obesity Class III (morbid): ≥40.0

Note: BMI less accurate in athletes (muscle mass), elderly, South Asian (risk at lower BMI)
Waist circumference: >94cm (men) / >80cm (women) = increased metabolic risk

Interventions:
- Lifestyle: diet + exercise + behavioural support first-line
- Pharmacotherapy: orlistat (inhibits fat absorption); GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide — highly effective)
- Bariatric surgery: if BMI ≥40, or ≥35 with comorbidities, failed non-surgical approach
  Options: Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy, adjustable gastric band
