CARDIAC RISK ASSESSMENT AND CHEST PAIN EVALUATION
==================================================

CHEST PAIN — EMERGENCY ASSESSMENT
====================================

Key Principle: chest pain is a medical emergency until proven otherwise.
Life-threatening causes must be excluded before other diagnoses are considered.

Life-Threatening Causes to Rule Out First:
1. Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS): STEMI, NSTEMI, Unstable Angina
2. Aortic Dissection
3. Pulmonary Embolism
4. Tension Pneumothorax
5. Pericardial Tamponade
6. Oesophageal Rupture (Boerhaave syndrome)

Immediate Assessment (within 10 minutes):
- 12-lead ECG
- Vital signs: BP (both arms if dissection suspected), HR, SpO2, respiratory rate
- IV access, bloods including troponin, FBC, coagulation, D-dimer
- Oxygen if SpO2 <94%
- Continuous cardiac monitoring

ACUTE CORONARY SYNDROME (ACS)
================================

ACS Spectrum:
1. ST-Elevation MI (STEMI): complete coronary occlusion; ECG shows ST elevation
2. Non-ST Elevation MI (NSTEMI): incomplete occlusion; elevated troponin, no ST elevation
3. Unstable Angina (UA): incomplete occlusion; no troponin rise, symptoms at rest

Symptoms of ACS:
- Central/crushing chest pain or pressure (classic)
- Radiation to left arm, jaw, neck, back, epigastrium
- Associated: diaphoresis, nausea, dyspnoea, vomiting, palpitations
- Atypical presentations (especially in women, elderly, diabetics):
  epigastric pain, dyspnoea only, fatigue, syncope, arm pain without chest pain

ECG Findings:
- STEMI: ST elevation ≥1mm in ≥2 contiguous leads (≥2mm in V1-V4)
  or new LBBB
- NSTEMI/UA: ST depression, T-wave inversion, or normal
- New Q waves: established infarct

Troponin Interpretation:
- Highly sensitive troponin (hsTn): positive >99th percentile of reference population
- Serial troponins: 0h and 3h (or 0h and 1h with hs-troponin protocol)
- Rise and fall pattern confirms AMI; sustained elevation = chronic myocardial injury

STEMI Management:
- Primary PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention): target door-to-balloon <90 minutes
- If PCI not available within 120 minutes: fibrinolysis (alteplase, tenecteplase)
- Dual antiplatelet therapy: aspirin 300mg + P2Y12 inhibitor (ticagrelor 180mg loading)
- Anticoagulation: heparin or bivalirudin

NSTEMI/UA Management:
- Antiplatelets: aspirin + ticagrelor or clopidogrel
- Anticoagulation: fondaparinux (preferred) or LMWH
- Risk stratification for coronary angiography (urgent vs. early vs. elective)
- Beta-blocker, statin, ACEi initiated early

HEART Score for NSTEMI Risk Stratification:
H: History (highly suspicious = 2, moderately suspicious = 1, slightly suspicious = 0)
E: ECG (LBBB or ST deviation = 2, non-specific changes = 1, normal = 0)
A: Age (≥65 = 2, 45-64 = 1, <45 = 0)
R: Risk factors (≥3 factors or known atherosclerosis = 2, 1-2 factors = 1, none = 0)
T: Troponin (>3x normal = 2, 1-3x normal = 1, ≤normal = 0)

Score 0-3: Low risk (1.7% MACE) — early discharge possible
Score 4-6: Moderate risk — observe, repeat troponin
Score ≥7: High risk (50-65% MACE) — early invasive strategy

STABLE ANGINA
==============
- Predictable chest pain with exertion/emotional stress; relieved by rest or GTN
- Due to fixed coronary stenosis limiting blood supply during increased demand

Management:
- Sublingual GTN spray: acute relief (spray under tongue, repeat 5 minutes if persists; call 999 if no relief after 3 doses)
- Beta-blockers: first-line for prophylaxis (atenolol, bisoprolol, metoprolol)
- CCBs: alternative or additive (amlodipine, diltiazem)
- Long-acting nitrates: isosorbide mononitrate; allow 12h nitrate-free period
- Ivabradine, ranolazine: if beta-blocker intolerant or inadequate control
- Antiplatelet: aspirin 75mg OD (secondary prevention)
- Statin: atorvastatin 40-80mg (high intensity) for all with established CVD
- Coronary revascularisation (PCI or CABG): if symptoms refractory to medical therapy

CARDIOVASCULAR RISK ASSESSMENT
================================

Cardiovascular Risk Factors:
Modifiable: hypertension, dyslipidaemia, diabetes, smoking, obesity, physical inactivity, unhealthy diet, excessive alcohol
Non-modifiable: age, male sex, family history (first-degree relative: <55 men, <65 women), ethnicity

Risk Stratification Tools:
- QRISK3 (UK): 10-year CVD risk calculator
- SCORE2 (Europe): fatal + non-fatal CVD events
- AHA/ACC Pooled Cohort Equations (US)

High-intensity statins (atorvastatin 40-80mg) indicated if:
- QRISK3 ≥10% (UK NICE guidance)
- Established ASCVD
- Primary prevention in diabetes ≥40 years
- Familial hypercholesterolaemia

Lipid Targets:
- High risk/established CVD: LDL-C <1.8 mmol/L or ≥50% reduction from baseline
- Very high risk (MI, stroke): LDL-C <1.4 mmol/L
- Primary prevention: LDL-C <3 mmol/L

AORTIC DISSECTION
==================
- Medical emergency; mortality 1–2% per hour if untreated in Type A
- Risk factors: hypertension, Marfan syndrome, bicuspid aortic valve, trauma, pregnancy

Classic symptoms: sudden onset, tearing or ripping pain; maximum at onset; radiates to back (descending)
Examination: BP discrepancy >20mmHg between arms; aortic regurgitation murmur; pulse deficits

Type A (involves ascending aorta): emergency surgical repair
Type B (descending only): medical management (pain control, BP reduction) unless complications

PULMONARY EMBOLISM (PE)
========================
Risk factors: DVT, prolonged immobilisation, surgery, malignancy, OCP, pregnancy, long-haul flights

Symptoms: acute dyspnoea (most common), pleuritic chest pain, haemoptysis, syncope
Signs: tachycardia, tachypnoea, oxygen desaturation, DVT signs (calf tenderness, swelling)

Wells Score for PE probability; D-dimer if low probability; CTPA if intermediate/high
ECG: sinus tachycardia (most common); S1Q3T3 (S wave lead I, Q wave lead III, T inversion lead III) — not specific

Treatment: anticoagulation — LMWH bridge to warfarin or DOAC (rivaroxaban, apixaban)
Massive PE with haemodynamic instability: thrombolysis (alteplase)
