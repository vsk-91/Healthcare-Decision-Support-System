DIABETES MELLITUS TYPE 2
========================

Definition
----------
Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycaemia
resulting from insulin resistance combined with relative insulin deficiency. It accounts for 90-95%
of all diabetes cases globally.

Diagnostic Criteria (ADA 2023)
-------------------------------
Any one of the following:
1. Fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) — after no caloric intake for ≥8 hours
2. 2-hour plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during OGTT (75g oral glucose load)
3. HbA1c ≥6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
4. Random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL with classic hyperglycaemia symptoms

Pre-diabetes:
- Impaired fasting glucose: 100–125 mg/dL
- Impaired glucose tolerance: 2h OGTT 140–199 mg/dL
- HbA1c: 5.7–6.4%

Classic Symptoms
----------------
- Polyuria (frequent urination)
- Polydipsia (excessive thirst)
- Polyphagia (excessive hunger)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue, blurred vision
- Recurrent infections (skin, UTI, genital candidiasis)
- Slow-healing wounds

Risk Factors
------------
- Obesity (BMI ≥30, especially central adiposity)
- Sedentary lifestyle
- Family history (first-degree relative)
- Previous gestational diabetes or PCOS
- Hypertension, dyslipidemia (metabolic syndrome)
- Ethnicity: South Asian, African, Hispanic higher risk
- Age ≥45 years

Investigations
--------------
- FPG and HbA1c for diagnosis and monitoring
- Lipid profile (cardiovascular risk)
- Urinalysis + urine ACR (albumin:creatinine ratio — microalbuminuria)
- Serum creatinine + eGFR (diabetic nephropathy)
- Thyroid function (comorbidity screening)
- Fundoscopy (retinopathy)
- Foot examination (neuropathy, vascular)
- ECG (cardiac risk)

Pharmacological Treatment
--------------------------
First-line: METFORMIN
- Mechanism: reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis, improves insulin sensitivity
- Dose: 500mg twice daily, titrate to 1000mg twice daily
- Contraindications: eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m², contrast procedures (hold 48h)
- Benefits: weight neutral, low hypoglycaemia risk, cheap, cardioprotective signals
- Side effects: GI upset (start low, titrate slowly; take with food)

Add-on therapies based on comorbidities:
1. GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (semaglutide, dulaglutide, liraglutide)
   - Preferred with established ASCVD, heart failure, CKD, or weight loss need
   - Significant cardiovascular and renal benefits
   - Side effects: nausea, vomiting; risk of pancreatitis

2. SGLT-2 Inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin)
   - Preferred in heart failure with reduced EF, CKD
   - Mechanisms: renal glucose excretion, reduce preload/afterload
   - Side effects: genital mycotic infections, DKA risk, UTI

3. DPP-4 Inhibitors (sitagliptin, saxagliptin)
   - Weight neutral, safe in CKD (dose adjust)
   - Modest HbA1c reduction

4. Sulfonylureas (gliclazide, glimepiride)
   - Inexpensive, effective; risk of hypoglycaemia and weight gain
   - Prefer gliclazide MR (lower hypoglycaemia risk)

5. Insulin therapy
   - Indicated: HbA1c >10%, symptomatic hyperglycaemia, failure of oral agents
   - Types: basal (glargine, detemir), rapid-acting (lispro, aspart), premixed

HbA1c Targets
--------------
- General target: <7.0% (53 mmol/mol)
- Individualise: <6.5% if newly diagnosed, low hypoglycaemia risk, no CVD
- Less stringent <8.0%: elderly, limited life expectancy, multiple comorbidities

Complications
-------------
Microvascular:
- Diabetic nephropathy: ACR >3 mg/mmol; manage with ACEi/ARB
- Diabetic retinopathy: annual fundoscopy; laser/anti-VEGF treatment
- Diabetic neuropathy: peripheral (glove-stocking numbness, burning), autonomic

Macrovascular:
- Coronary artery disease (leading cause of death)
- Stroke
- Peripheral artery disease (foot ulcers, amputation risk)

Diabetic Foot:
- Annual foot exam mandatory
- Monofilament testing for neuropathy
- Inspect for callus, ulcers, deformity, ischaemia
- Multidisciplinary foot team for ulcer management

Lifestyle Management
--------------------
- Medical nutrition therapy: balanced diet, carbohydrate awareness, reduce refined sugars
- Physical activity: 150 min/week moderate intensity aerobic + resistance training
- Weight management: 5-10% weight loss significantly improves glycaemic control
- Smoking cessation: critical for macrovascular risk reduction
- Alcohol: moderation; educate on hypoglycaemia risk with insulin/sulfonylureas
