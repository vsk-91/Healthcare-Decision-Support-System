HYPERTENSION MANAGEMENT
========================

Definition and Classification
------------------------------
Hypertension is defined as a sustained blood pressure at or above 130/80 mmHg (ACC/AHA 2017 guidelines)
or 140/90 mmHg (ESC/ESH 2018 guidelines). It is the leading modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular
morbidity and mortality worldwide.

Stages:
- Elevated BP: 120-129 / <80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: 130-139 / 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: ≥140 / ≥90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: >180 / >120 mmHg

Clinical Presentation
---------------------
Most patients with hypertension are asymptomatic. When symptomatic:
- Headache (occipital, morning)
- Blurred vision
- Dizziness
- Epistaxis in severe cases
- Hypertensive urgency/emergency: severe headache, confusion, chest pain, dyspnea

Target Organ Damage Assessment
--------------------------------
Physicians must assess for end-organ damage:
- Heart: LVH on ECG/echo, heart failure, coronary artery disease
- Brain: stroke, TIA, hypertensive encephalopathy
- Kidney: CKD, proteinuria, elevated creatinine
- Eyes: hypertensive retinopathy (fundoscopy)
- Peripheral arteries: ankle-brachial index

Investigations
--------------
- BP measurement (both arms, sitting, standing)
- Urinalysis for proteinuria
- Serum creatinine, electrolytes, glucose, lipid profile
- ECG (LVH assessment)
- Echocardiogram if indicated
- Renal ultrasound if secondary hypertension suspected

Pharmacological Treatment — First-Line Agents
----------------------------------------------
1. ACE Inhibitors (ramipril, lisinopril, enalapril)
   - Preferred in CKD with proteinuria, diabetes, heart failure
   - Monitor: serum creatinine, potassium; avoid in pregnancy

2. ARBs (losartan, valsartan, candesartan)
   - Alternative to ACEi if ACE cough occurs
   - Similar indications to ACEi
   - Monitor: serum creatinine, potassium

3. Calcium Channel Blockers (amlodipine, nifedipine)
   - Preferred in elderly, isolated systolic hypertension, angina
   - Side effects: peripheral edema, flushing

4. Thiazide Diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide, indapamide)
   - Cost-effective, preferred in black patients and elderly
   - Monitor: potassium, uric acid, glucose

Beta-Blockers (bisoprolol, metoprolol):
- Not first-line for uncomplicated hypertension
- Preferred if concurrent: angina, heart failure with reduced EF, post-MI, AF rate control

Lifestyle Modification (Non-pharmacological)
--------------------------------------------
- DASH diet: high in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy; limits saturated fat
- Sodium restriction: target <2.3 g/day (ideal <1.5 g/day)
- Weight reduction: each 1 kg weight loss reduces BP by ~1 mmHg
- Aerobic exercise: at least 150 min/week of moderate intensity
- Alcohol moderation: <14 units/week for men, <7 for women
- Smoking cessation: does not directly lower BP but reduces cardiovascular risk

BP Targets
----------
- General population: <130/80 mmHg (ACC/AHA) or <140/90 (ESC)
- Elderly ≥65 years: <130/80 mmHg if tolerated
- CKD without proteinuria: <140/90
- CKD with proteinuria/diabetes: <130/80
- Post-stroke: <130/80

Secondary Hypertension — When to Suspect
------------------------------------------
- Age <30 years without family history
- Resistant to 3 drugs at adequate doses
- Sudden onset or rapid worsening
- Hypokalemia without diuretics
- Causes: renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, phaeochromocytoma,
  Cushing's syndrome, thyroid disease, obstructive sleep apnoea

Monitoring
----------
- Self-monitoring at home: target <135/85 mmHg
- Ambulatory BP monitoring: preferred for white-coat and masked hypertension
- Follow-up: every 3 months until controlled, then 6-12 monthly
