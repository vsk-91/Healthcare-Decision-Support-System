GASTRO-OESOPHAGEAL REFLUX DISEASE (GERD) AND PEPTIC DISORDERS
===============================================================

GASTRO-OESOPHAGEAL REFLUX DISEASE (GERD)
==========================================

Definition
----------
GERD is defined as symptoms or complications resulting from the reflux of gastric contents into the
oesophagus. It is one of the most prevalent gastrointestinal conditions globally, affecting 10–30%
of Western populations.

Pathophysiology
---------------
Primary mechanism: transient lower oesophageal sphincter (LOS) relaxations
Contributing factors:
- Hiatus hernia (gastro-oesophageal junction displaced above diaphragm)
- Impaired oesophageal clearance (reduced peristalsis)
- Delayed gastric emptying
- Obesity (increased intra-abdominal pressure)
- Medications reducing LOS tone: nitrates, calcium channel blockers, theophylline, anticholinergics

Typical Symptoms
-----------------
- Heartburn: burning sensation in epigastrium/retrosternal area, worse after meals, bending, lying flat
- Regurgitation: bitter/sour taste in mouth
- Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing — if stricture or eosinophilic oesophagitis)
- Odynophagia (pain on swallowing)

Atypical/Extra-oesophageal Symptoms:
- Chronic cough (especially nocturnal)
- Hoarseness/voice changes (laryngopharyngeal reflux)
- Asthma exacerbation
- Dental erosions
- Globus sensation

ALARM SYMPTOMS requiring urgent endoscopy:
- Dysphagia (progressive)
- Unintentional weight loss
- Haematemesis or melaena
- Iron deficiency anaemia
- Persistent vomiting
- Palpable mass or lymphadenopathy
- Age >55 with new onset dyspepsia

Investigations
--------------
- Clinical diagnosis in typical uncomplicated GERD (no need for routine investigation)
- Upper GI endoscopy (OGD): for alarm symptoms, failure to respond to PPI, or prolonged symptoms
  - Findings: oesophagitis (graded by Los Angeles classification A–D), Barrett's oesophagus, stricture
- 24-hour pH monitoring: gold standard for diagnosis when endoscopy normal
- Oesophageal manometry: if dysphagia; assess peristalsis and LOS pressure
- H. pylori testing: test and treat in dyspepsia (urea breath test, stool antigen)

Pharmacological Treatment
--------------------------
Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) — First-line:
- Omeprazole 20mg once daily before breakfast (or lansoprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole)
- Duration: 4–8 weeks for acute symptom control; long-term if symptoms recur
- Double dose: if standard dose ineffective, or erosive oesophagitis grade C/D
- Risks of long-term PPI use: hypomagnesaemia, C. difficile infection, osteoporosis,
  renal disease, small bowel bacterial overgrowth

H2 Receptor Antagonists (H2RAs):
- Ranitidine (withdrawn due to NDMA contamination); famotidine, cimetidine
- Less effective than PPIs but useful for: breakthrough symptoms, nocturnal acid suppression,
  when PPI contraindicated

Antacids/Alginate Preparations:
- Gaviscon (sodium alginate + antacid): forms protective raft above gastric contents
- For occasional/mild symptoms; rapid relief but short-acting

Lifestyle and Dietary Modifications (Non-pharmacological)
----------------------------------------------------------
- Weight loss (most effective intervention): target BMI <25
- Elevate head of bed 15–20 cm (for nocturnal/supine symptoms)
- Avoid lying down 3 hours after meals
- Reduce dietary triggers: fatty/fried foods, spicy food, chocolate, peppermint, citrus
- Reduce caffeine, carbonated drinks, alcohol
- Smaller, more frequent meals
- Avoid tight-fitting clothing
- Stop smoking (weakens LOS)

HELICOBACTER PYLORI (H. PYLORI) INFECTION
==========================================
- Gram-negative organism colonising gastric mucosa
- Prevalence: 50% worldwide; higher in developing countries
- Association: peptic ulcer disease, gastric adenocarcinoma, MALT lymphoma

Diagnosis:
- Urea breath test (UBT): preferred; high sensitivity/specificity
- Stool antigen test: equivalent accuracy; useful post-treatment
- Endoscopic biopsy + rapid urease test (CLO test): if OGD performed
- Serology: not recommended (cannot distinguish active from past infection)

Eradication Treatment:
Standard 7-day triple therapy:
- PPI (omeprazole 20mg BD) + amoxicillin 1g BD + clarithromycin 500mg BD
- 7–14 days (14 days where clarithromycin resistance >15%)

If penicillin allergy:
- PPI + metronidazole 400mg BD + clarithromycin 500mg BD

Quadruple therapy (if clarithromycin resistance or treatment failure):
- PPI + bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline for 10–14 days

PEPTIC ULCER DISEASE (PUD)
===========================
- Gastric ulcer: typically lesser curve; associated with H. pylori and NSAIDs
- Duodenal ulcer: most common; strongly associated with H. pylori

Symptoms:
- Epigastric pain: burning or gnawing
- Gastric ulcer: pain worsened by eating
- Duodenal ulcer: pain relieved by eating, worsened 2–3h post-meal, nocturnal pain

Complications:
- Haemorrhage (haematemesis, melaena) — most common complication
- Perforation (sudden-onset severe generalised abdominal pain, peritonitis)
- Gastric outlet obstruction (projectile vomiting)

Management:
- H. pylori eradication (if positive)
- PPI therapy 4–8 weeks (gastric ulcer); 4 weeks (duodenal ulcer)
- Stop causative NSAIDs; if must continue: co-prescribe PPI
- Repeat endoscopy at 6–8 weeks for gastric ulcer (to confirm healing and exclude malignancy)
- Surgery: reserved for complications (perforation, haemorrhage unresponsive to endoscopic therapy)
